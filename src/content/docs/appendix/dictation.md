---
title: Dictation
description: Why speaking your request beats typing it, and how to fix poor accuracy.
---

Most people give AI thin context for a dull reason. Typing a full brief is work, so they type one line and get a one-line result. Speaking removes that effort. It is the easiest habit you can add to get better answers.

## Why it works

You can talk about three to four times faster than you type. It also makes detail cheap. When you type, you trim things to save effort. When you talk, you naturally mention who it is for, the limits, an example, and what to avoid. That is exactly the context the model was missing.

Twenty seconds of rambling out loud usually beats two minutes of careful typing, and it feels easier.

## "I tried it and it was awful"

That is fair. Many people remember the old phone version, where voice typing mangled every third word unless you spoke slowly in a quiet room. That reputation was earned.

Modern AI dictation is different. Current speech models are fast and accurate, even with an accent, at a normal pace, with some background noise. If your last try was a couple of years ago, you are judging a tool that has since been replaced. Try a current one before you give up.

## Still inaccurate? Check the microphone first

Here is a trap few people know about. When the text comes out wrong, people blame the AI. Often the microphone is the real problem. Many "noise-cancelling" headsets squash and filter your voice so much that the model receives muddy audio, and no model transcribes mud well.

From personal experience: I turned off a noise-cancelling headset mic and switched to a good standalone microphone. Accuracy jumped a lot, with no software change at all. If your results are poor, try another mic before you decide dictation is not for you. The model is rarely the weak link. The audio going in usually is.

## How to do it

- Turn on your tool's AI voice input, or use a current dictation app, not the old phone kind.
- Talk the task through: what you want, who it is for, and what would make the result wrong. Do not tidy it up. Messy and complete beats neat and thin.
- Let the AI clean up after you. A good habit is to ask it to restate your ramble as a clear brief and to ask about anything missing before it starts.

## See the difference

**Typed one-liner:** "write a launch email for saved filters." The result is generic and takes several rounds of fixes.

**Spoken in about twenty seconds:** "Write a short launch email to our existing power users about saved filters. Keep it warm but not hyped. It has to say it is free and works on mobile, but do not promise a mobile app. Match the tone of last month's dark mode email. I want to send it with only light edits." The first draft is usable.

Same person, same task, about the same effort. The only change was speaking instead of typing.

## A small note

This sits in the appendix on purpose. Dictation is a tool, not an idea to master. The idea is [give it the right context](../../theory/give-it-the-right-context/), and dictation is the easiest way to do it.
