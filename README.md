# AI Fluency

Tools change. Providers change. The skills don't. A short, tool-agnostic guide to using AI well:
how it really works, how to direct it, how to check it, and how to stop overpaying for it.

**Read it here: <https://sgarcia-dev.github.io/ai-fluency-course/>**

## Where this came from

I first built this for my friends at Reddit as a hackathon project. It turned out to be useful well
beyond one company, so I rewrote it from scratch and put it in the open.

**Why I built it.** I kept seeing the same pattern. Most AI training teaches one tool: "Cursor 101",
"how to use X". Those courses go stale in a few months, because the models, tools and providers keep
changing. Meanwhile the problems that actually hurt people never get taught:

- People reach for the most expensive model because they assume expensive means good. It burns money,
  and faster, cheaper models that would do the job sit unused.
- People use tools they don't understand, so they get frustrated or misuse them.
- Being shown how to set up an MCP server or a "skill" doesn't make anyone feel in control. Knowing
  what it is and why it matters does.
- Every switch to a new provider feels like starting over, so people stop investing at all.

I think all of that is one problem. We teach tools, but the skill that lasts is knowing how to direct
AI, check its work, and pay the right amount for it, in any tool.

**Why it matters to me.** The gap between people who get a lot out of AI and people who don't is
rarely about talent or interest. It is about time. Most people never got a spare afternoon to learn
how this stuff really works, so they avoid it or quietly overpay for it. That isn't their fault. I
had the time and the curiosity to dig in, and this is my way of handing that back.

The part I most want people to take away sounds too good to be true: using AI well is not a
trade-off. Smarter habits and faster, cheaper models get you better results, sooner, for less money,
all at once. Lower cost is the side effect, not the goal. The goal is to feel in control.

This is meant to raise the bar for everyone, from the curious beginner to the busy expert who has
never had time to sharpen up. If you already run your own agent setups, most of it will be review,
and I would love your help making it better.
